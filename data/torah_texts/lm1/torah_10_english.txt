Torah 10 teaches that harsh divine judgments can be sweetened through dancing and hand-clapping. God's greatness is fully manifest when even the nations acknowledge Him, which is achieved through the spiritual quality of Jacob, who elevated prayer from a "mountain" (remote) to a "house" (accessible to all). Only true tzaddikim can achieve this elevation. Arrogant individuals who refuse to seek the tzaddik represent the force of idolatry; pride is equated with idolatry. Fasting alone does not uproot deep-seated desires — the teaching uses the allegory of a sack full of holes where desires remain even after emptying. The remedy for pride/idolatry is drawing close to the true tzaddik, whose spirit breaks the haughty spirit. This spirit flows through the joints of arms and legs, producing clapping and dancing. Through dancing, pride is annulled; through clapping, heresy is nullified and faith increases. The Torah itself has "hands" (revealed dimension) and "feet" (hidden/esoteric dimension), both flowing from the "heart." The teaching maps onto Purim: Haman represents idolatry; Mordechai corresponds to hands/revealed Torah; Esther to feet/hidden Torah. Through them Israel re-accepted Torah, nullifying idolatry. The Talmudic tale of Korah's company being swallowed is decoded: Korah embodied heresy/arrogance; the declaration "Moses and his Torah are true" represents the ultimate acknowledgment even the most estranged must make. Practical takeaways: seek a genuine tzaddik; humility is essential; joyful physical worship (dancing/clapping) is a profound spiritual mechanism; the goal is making God's presence universally known.
