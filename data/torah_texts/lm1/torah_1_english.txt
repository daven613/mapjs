Torah 1 of Likutey Moharan opens with the verse from Psalms 119:1, "Happy are those who are wholesome in their way, who walk in the Torah of God," and proceeds to construct an intricate teaching about how Torah study causes prayers and requests to be accepted, elevates the grace and esteem of the Jewish people, and subdues the evil inclination. The central thesis is that through Torah study, all prayers and petitions are received and answered. Rabbi Nachman explains that in the current era, the true grace and importance of the Jewish people has fallen, and honor and prestige have shifted to the nations. However, the Torah restores this grace, because the Torah is called "a loving hind and a graceful doe" (Proverbs 5:19), which the Talmud (Eruvin 54b) interprets to mean that the Torah bestows grace upon those who study it. This restored grace is what causes prayers to find acceptance. The teaching develops its core spiritual psychology: every Jewish person must constantly look into the inner wisdom and intellect embedded in all things, connecting oneself to the divine intelligence present in every aspect of reality. This intellect is described as a great light that illuminates all of one's paths. Rabbi Nachman maps these concepts onto a symbolic system involving the sun, the moon, and Hebrew letters. The intellect functions like the sun, illuminating all paths. Wisdom is linked to the Hebrew letter Chet (life/vitality), and must be received through the letter Nun (sovereignty/Malkhut). When properly combined, these form the word Chen (grace). Each person must strengthen the holy sovereignty over the wicked sovereignty through Torah study, which empowers the holy Malkhut (Nun) to receive from wisdom (Chet), creating grace that makes prayers effective. The teaching interprets a Talmudic tale allegorically: a wave threatening a ship represents the evil inclination trying to drown the grace of the Jewish people; the clubs inscribed with divine names represent Torah study that subdues it. The practical takeaway is threefold: cultivate the habit of seeking divine wisdom in every experience, engage in vigorous Torah study as the primary instrument for spiritual growth, and understand that the resulting grace causes one's words and prayers to be heard and accepted.
