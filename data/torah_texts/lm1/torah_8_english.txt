Torah 8 is built on Zechariah's vision of a golden menorah. The teaching opens with the assertion that a Jewish person's sigh is exceedingly precious because it constitutes the completion of whatever is lacking — since the world was created through breath/spirit, a sigh draws down fresh life-force. The essential life-force is received from the tzaddik and leading sage, who is bound to Torah where the spirit of life resides. The wicked, who speak against the tzaddik, receive a "stormy wind" from an impure source associated with Esau — powerful briefly but ultimately destructive. The tzaddik, by drawing down the spirit of life, simultaneously atones for sin. Only a "complete tzaddik" — one who has fully separated evil from good within all four elements (fire, wind, water, earth) — may confront the wicked. This separation is accomplished through Torah study (especially halakhic decisors who clarify permitted from forbidden) and prayer (which provides the intellectual capacity for deep study). Prayer represents creation-in-potential while Torah represents creation-in-actuality; together they enable separating good from evil. The four fringes of tzitzit correspond to the spirit from four directions, subduing Esau's stormy wind. The holy white tallit opposes Esau's red garment of impurity. Zechariah's menorah is Torah; the bowl is wisdom from prayer; the seven lamps are souls nourished by Torah; the two olive trees are the Tree of Life and Knowledge. "Not by might but by My spirit" — through Torah and prayer the tzaddik draws down the holy spirit, filling all deficiencies and subduing opposition.
