Torah 2 centers on a passage from the Zohar's Sifra DiTzniuta about the spirit of life being drawn to the Messiah. The teaching establishes that the primary weapon of the Messiah is prayer, connected to the nose/smell (Isaiah 48:9, Isaiah 11:3). One can only acquire this weapon through the spiritual quality of Joseph — guarding the covenant of sexual purity. Prayer has two dimensions (praise of God and petition for needs), like a double-edged sword. Possessing the sword of prayer is not enough; one must wield it with precision through the quality of mishpat (justice), the balanced center between extremes. Justice is attained through giving charity, which mirrors divine judgment by redistributing wealth. This is why one gives charity before praying. The teaching addresses distracting thoughts during prayer, identifying their root cause as corruption of justice. Each person must direct their prayer to connect with the righteous leader (tzaddik) of the generation, who embodies Moses-Messiah. Just as only Moses could assemble the Tabernacle, only the tzaddik can properly arrange prayers. Torah studied with intention to fulfill contains sparks of souls that become clothed within prayer. The teaching interprets the Talmudic tale of a great fish allegorically: in exile, God has hidden His face, but the righteous of every generation are assembling prayers and building the stature of the Shekhinah. The teaching presents an integrated system: sexual purity gives access to prayer; charity gives access to justice for precise prayer; Torah study fills prayer with soul-sparks; and all prayers must be channeled through the tzaddik, who assembles them into the complete Shekhinah structure, ultimately leading to redemption through God's grace.
