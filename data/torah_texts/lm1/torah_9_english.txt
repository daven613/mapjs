Torah 9 teaches that all vitality is received through prayer, and one must pray with full strength so that strength is renewed within prayer itself. There are twelve tribes corresponding to twelve constellations, each with its own prayer gate. When a tribe prays through its gate, it activates its constellation, causing sustenance below. This explains why sustenance and finding a marriage partner are "as difficult as the splitting of the Red Sea" — the Sea split into twelve paths for twelve tribes, mirroring twelve prayer gates. Great merit is required to direct prayer through one's proper gate. The patriarch Jacob, encompassing all twelve tribes, could direct each prayer to its proper root. When one stands to pray, alien thoughts surround him in darkness; the key to finding an opening is truth, which draws God's presence as illumination. Noah's Ark symbolizes this: a "tzohar" (light-source) in the ark represents truthful speech that radiates light even in darkness. Every person must bind prayer to the tzaddik of the generation, who knows how to direct each prayer to its proper gate. The teaching connects prayer, faith, miracles, and the Land of Israel: prayer operates through miracles; the Land of Israel is the essential place of miracles; denial of miracles damages prayer and causes exile. Practical takeaways: pray with total investment; cultivate truth in speech; attach to the tzaddik; study Torah as prerequisite for prayer; affirm divine providence rather than reducing everything to natural causation.
