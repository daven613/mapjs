Torah 6 teaches that every person must minimize their own honor and increase God's honor. Divine honor is attained through repentance, whose essence is remaining silent when shamed. Before repentance, a person has no true existence; through repentance they enter the state of "I will be." The remedy is transforming "dam" (blood/evil inclination) into "dom" (silence). When one fulfills "Be silent before the Lord," God fells one's enemies and the evil inclination is diminished. A striking idea follows: one must continually repent because even the act of confession cannot be performed with a perfectly pure heart, and as spiritual perception deepens, earlier understanding appears coarse by comparison. This creates an endless ascending spiral of repentance identified with the World to Come. Two forms of expertise are needed: "expert in running" (never stopping at spiritual achievements) and "expert in returning" (never despairing from spiritual falls). The entire teaching maps onto the Hebrew letter Aleph (upper yod = Crown/repentance/Moses/sun, lower yod = silence/Joshua/moon, connecting vav = firmament/Jacob). Through silence one attains the Aleph and becomes a true human being. The teaching also emphasizes judging every person favorably — even those who insult you — as this constructs the spiritual Crown. Practical takeaways: flee personal honor; absorb insult in silence; repentance is never finished; cultivate dual expertise in spiritual ascent and recovery from falls; judge everyone favorably.
