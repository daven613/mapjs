Torah 5 is built on "With trumpets and the sound of the shofar, make a joyous noise before the Lord" (Psalms 98:6). It opens with the principle that every person bears responsibility for the world's repair and must pray on behalf of others. Prayer operates in two modes: before a decree, one prays normally; after a decree, the righteous must "clothe" their prayer in stories to bypass accusatory angels. Discerning whether a decree has been issued depends on whether one performs mitzvot with overwhelming joy, wanting no reward — not even the World to Come. The teaching maps this onto Moses's luminous prophecy versus other prophets' dim prophecy. The mitzvot form a complete spiritual structure vitalizing World, Year, and Soul. God's joy is clothed within the mitzvot, so one who enters the joy of the mitzvah enters God's own joy. True joy requires straightening the heart, achieved through "thunder" — the powerful voice in prayer striking the mind. Fear of Heaven generates the power behind this voice. The shofar (ram's horn of Isaac/Gevurah) embodies this principle. For the mechanism to work, the mind must be free of foreign wisdoms and impure thoughts ("chametz"). Disputes among the truly righteous serve as personal rebuke — indicating one's own mind has been blemished. The teaching emphasizes uniting Gevurah (severity) with Chesed (lovingkindness), and fear with love. Practical takeaways: take personal responsibility for the world; perform mitzvot with pure joy; invest full vocal power in prayer; keep the mind pure; and understand that fear and love must be united.
