Torah 7 teaches that exile stems from a lack of faith, and redemption comes through restoring it. Faith corresponds to prayer, which corresponds to miracles transcending nature. These are rooted most fully in the Land of Israel. Abraham's question "How shall I know?" damaged faith regarding the Land, causing the Egyptian exile. All subsequent exiles similarly constrict Israel and suppress the miraculous. The Messiah will come when heresy (denial of miracles) ends and faith spreads. One cannot arrive at genuine faith except through truth, and truth comes through drawing close to the righteous and following their counsel. Receiving counsel from a tzaddik is likened to a sacred spiritual union; receiving counsel from the wicked is like Eve receiving the serpent's counsel, introducing contamination. At Sinai, the 613 commandments constituted holy counsels that purified Israel. The commandment of tzitzit (fringes) protects against falling into wicked counsel by guarding the eyes and sexual purity. The teaching constructs a precise chain: tzitzit → protection from impure counsel → receiving the tzaddik's counsel → truth → faith → redemption. Prayer is also conducive to memory, since faith (prayer/miracles) is the opposite of forgetfulness (natural routine). Gazing at tzitzit is a spiritual remedy for the sick.
